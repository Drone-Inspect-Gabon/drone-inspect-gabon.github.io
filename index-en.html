03 — Geodetic foundation

WGS-84: ensuring consistency, not just recording coordinates

The World Geodetic System 1984 is the reference horizontal geodetic datum for international air navigation. A rigorous WGS-84 project ensures that critical aeronautical points are determined, controlled and documented within a reference system consistent with navigation systems.

  • Establishing or checking a network of geodetic reference points.
  • Coordinates of runway thresholds, ends, characteristic points and navigation aids.
  • Transformation and control of historical data.
  • Full traceability: methods, datum, transformations, quality.
  • Data compatible with AIS/AIM requirements.

04 — Why LiDAR?

The 3D geometry of the environment, measured directly.

On an airfield, LiDAR quickly delivers a dense, usable point cloud that distinguishes terrain, vegetation, buildings, masts and any object likely to interact with protection surfaces or flight procedures.

The drone platform delivers a resolution that conventional satellite imagery cannot match — precisely where obstacle clearance margins are decided.

Lubumbashi is presented as an update, review and optimisation study — FZQA already has RNP RWY 07 and RNP RWY 25 approaches, as well as RNAV departure and arrival procedures. A February 2026 NavData notice also flags that the final transition of several RWY 07 procedures, including RNP RWY 07, is temporarily unusable due to a 600 m displaced threshold — reinforcing the case for a pilot project built on updated geospatial data.

Pilot objectives
  • 01Geodetic and documentary stocktake of available data.
  • 02Checking and completing the required WGS-84 data.
  • 03LiDAR campaign over the airfield's priority areas.
  • 04Terrain / obstacle model and identification of critical objects.
 
  • 05Complementary coverage from qualified satellite data.
  • 06Re-assessment of constraints affecting RNP RWY 07 and RWY 25.
  • 07Trajectory and minima optimisation scenarios, subject to ICAO criteria.
  • 08A clear demonstrator for the Authority to evaluate before wider rollout.

Covers the full range of conventional and PBN (Performance Based Navigation) procedures, with a major software upgrade every year to track evolving ICAO criteria.

A tool-based approach, not a fully automated one

Rather than relying on fully automated algorithms, the software follows a tool-based philosophy: the designer keeps control of their expertise and judgement to build procedures suited to each context, while preserving safety and efficiency. This approach also speeds up validation — only new tools need revalidating with each update, unlike a fully automated system where the whole package must be revalidated.
